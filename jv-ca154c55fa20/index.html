Who is who, and what each brings

Not a vendor. Not a customer. A studio admission.

◆ The discipline underneath every clause

Rights ⊥ Costs ⊥ Capital ⊥ Upside — four ledgers kept orthogonal until a contract explicitly connects them. No contribution silently changes economic form. Nobody is paid in your ownership. Temporary scarcity creates temporary claims, never permanent dispossession. Where you see something left open in this document, it is open on purpose, and it says who closes it.

If the engine earns nothing, you owe nothing.

YOUR MODEL, READ. Your workbook books StarHub at 20% on the Assumptions tab, carried as a cost of revenue in the monthly P&L. Two things follow. The rate is 30%, not 20% — and we would rather correct that with you than have a diligence pass find your own operator's share understated in your own model. And it does not belong in cost of revenue: as a venture split in its own ledger it sits below your operating result, which is both more accurate and considerably kinder to the margin you show an investor. Your gross reconciles to the dollar and is carried unchanged.

◆ Launch or Growth — the scope is yours to choose

LAUNCH — $50,000 cash / $65,000 carried. Everything built to date: the site, the funnel, the brand system, the document library, the growth plan, 90 days of support.

GROWTH — $90,000 cash / $117,000 carried. All of Launch, plus six months of us running it: the e-commerce build, the fan-funding campaign, the migration off your current email platform, and the marketing.

Items 5 and 6 on your own agenda — selling the trip, the e-commerce steps, the fan-funding campaign, the back end — are Growth. What exists today is Launch. The path we would suggest: begin at Launch and upgrade to Growth when funding lands. Both are carried either way, so the difference triggers on the same event, and the six months of marketing then begins when there is money to market with.

IF LAUNCH IS CHOSEN, THE GROWTH WORK IS OUT OF SCOPE. Marketing, e-commerce build and campaign operation are quoted separately when you want them. This sentence exists because the alternative — doing the larger work inside the smaller price because nobody wrote down which was which — is how good partnerships quietly go sour.

◆ Why the setup fee is carried at all

You are opening doors: introductions to investors for the mythOS raise, and referrals to people in your circle who need the work we do. Those introductions are why we carry the fee rather than ask for it at signature. Neither is an obligation and neither carries a quota — you cannot promise what an investor decides. But they are real consideration, they are part of what you bring to this, and they are named here rather than left as an understanding.

◆ The budget determines the raise — never the reverse

The ~$1M figure in circulation is your capitalization hypothesis, and it has been treated as exactly that at every step: never adopted, never quietly used as a target. Phase II produces the real number by costing the authorized schedule honestly. If it is larger, that is what the raise becomes. If it is smaller, nobody raises money they do not need. A budget written backwards from a raise target is how films run out of money in post.

section 06 · Instrument III — How the SPV Protects You
The clauses that matter most when money arrives

The raise is an output. The waterfall comes before any percentage.

The SPV activates only when the capitalization requirement falls out of the Phase II budget — whatever it sums to. Until then no raise number is quoted, by us or on your behalf. These four clauses are written into it, and each one exists because of a way creators routinely lose films.

Clause What it means for you
Deferral accrues; capitalization cures it Anyone who defers payment before close gets paid at financing close or per agreed draws. Deferred work converts into risk capital ONLY by express election — never silently. Nobody accumulates a claim on your film by quietly working unpaid.
Waterfall before percentages Tier order is fixed first (approved expenses → capital recoupment → premium → residual pool → regeneration), and every participation names its tier. “Percentage of net” is banned unless “net” points at a defined tier — which is the clause that makes Hollywood accounting impossible here.
StarHub's back end is earned, not inherited Our participation is justified by continuing contribution — packaging, risk, financing role, the platform — never merely by having once deferred payment.
Mandate completion When the mandate completes, rights revert, claims settle, and the vehicle dissolves. The temporary economic organism does not outlive its purpose.
